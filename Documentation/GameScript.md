# Last Minute Courier — Game Script

Development version v0.8, November 8, 2026. Rules and core mechanics describe implemented behavior. Structure checked against lecture deck 3: slides 19-23 distinguish gameplay modes and shell screens; slide 55 asks for a non-technical rules script. Player timing and response remain measured acceptance tasks.

## Background and goal

A campus film screening is about to begin. The courier carries the three things that make the evening possible: coffee for the audience, a lens for the projector, and the reel for the clocktower screening. A pocket device borrows seconds from marked machinery while the rest of the world keeps moving. The goal is to complete all three deliveries and open the cinema.

## Learning goals

Learn moving, sprinting and looking; physically jump over a low obstacle; slide under a beam that is too low to walk under and too tall to hop; recognize blue clock tags and learn that Q extends a useful gap in machinery movement. Climbing (a jump into a ledge between knee and reach height pulls the courier up) is taught on the first crate wall of the coffee route, with a hint, before it is needed anywhere else. The same abilities are then combined in new ways: Q slows a closing shutter so a slide fits under it, slows a turnstile so its two arms (one to jump, one to slide under) can be read, and slows lifts and a shuttle for boarding; climbing becomes a staircase up to a market roof; sprint plus a springboard replaces the last lift. Alternative arcades and ramps let the player trade route length for precision. Gold hourglasses (+1 Q use each) and lines of gold sparks (25 = +1 Q use, close calls add more) sit mainly on the bolder lines, so using an ability well funds the next one. No new input is introduced during a timed delivery.

## Rules and modes

Three untimed opening cards explain the evening, the ability and the controls (drawn as key caps). A practice yard teaches sprinting, jumping, sliding and Q. WASD moves, mouse looks, Shift (held) sprints, Space jumps (and climbs a ledge it meets), Ctrl slides, Q slows marked devices, E interacts, C recenters, H opens help, Esc pauses, and R asks whether to restart the current unfinished order.

Walking up to a desk opens its brief (E reopens it after it is closed). The brief lists the parcel, destination, time limit and Q uses; E again or Enter accepts, Esc or Back closes it. Only acceptance starts the order clock. Orders have 40, 120 and 130 seconds and 3, 4 and 5 uses of borrowed time. Each use slows blue devices to 0.25 speed for at most 3 seconds, followed by a 1.5-second recharge; the remaining uses are shown as a count and pips beside the Q key. Practice is unlimited. The courier and order clock do not slow. Pausing freezes gameplay; releasing Q while paused is resolved on resume.

A cart knocks the courier aside with a short stagger (no teleport); during the lens delivery each knock or fall also cracks the lens case a little, and the third knock fails that order. Falling into a pond or off a walkway returns the courier to the last grounded checkpoint with a short fade and an explanation ("Splash!", "You dropped off the walkway"). Hedges mark the edge of the campus. The current order remains active. Timeout or a cracked lens offers retry and route help. R asks before restarting only the unfinished order, its devices, uses and cells; earlier deliveries and stamps remain. Delivered and final states cannot be undone by R. Play Again starts a fresh run.

## Scene and interaction sequence

1. Read the three cards, select an optional language, and practice in a safe yard with no delivery clock: sprint to a marker, jump a step, slide under a striped beam, cross a cart lane.
2. Accept coffee at the parcel desk. Cross cart traffic or use the arcade; climb the crate wall (jump at it) or walk round it; beat the first rolling shutter by sliding under it before it shuts or waiting for it to reopen. E delivers at the coffee stand and its warm light turns on.
3. Read and accept the lens at the same stand. Climb a second crate wall on the garden approach, use or bypass the first lift, cross the raised beds and stepping stones (or use the side route), clear a turnstile (jump the low arm, slide under the high one), then reach the projection booth via the second lift or ramp and through the booth's shutter. The projector appears after delivery.
4. Read and accept the reel at the booth. Navigate traffic (or climb crates onto the market roof for sparks and an hourglass), slide under the market shutter, ride or bypass a horizontal shuttle, clear a wider turnstile and two lanes, then reach the upper walk by the lift or by sprinting over a springboard. Slide under the clocktower gate and negotiate planters. Follow the central cinema aisle, then turn left toward the roofless clocktower console and its orange square. The screening lights and landscape screen reveal; a cinema ticket shows three stamps and the run statistics.

## Feedback and presentation

Menus are courier slips: a coloured header band, paper body, perforation and key-cap buttons. The in-play HUD uses compact dark plates in the corners: order and destination (top left), the delivery clock that turns red and pulses under 20 seconds plus a rotating compass arrow and distance (top centre), the Q key and charge meter that flashes red when Q is pressed without charge (bottom left), and a single contextual line with the E prompt (bottom centre). Lilita One sets display type and Nunito interface text; Chinese, Japanese and Korean use Noto Sans CJK. Failed actions have explicit messages and recovery. Each completed delivery changes the world, plays a stamp cue and preserves progress. Warm sunset, stationary soft clouds and distant hills support orientation. A 48-second original musical base gains synchronized strings after coffee and bells after the lens; all three layers and interaction sounds share the sound switch. English is the startup language; six optional languages are selected from one menu.

## Comfort and accessibility

Adjust field of view and sensitivity; optionally lock camera direction; use C to aim toward the route. No camera shake, head bob or motion blur; the only automatic camera change is a gentle widening of at most 9 degrees while sprinting or sliding. Reading never consumes the order budget. Pausing suspends play, retries preserve earlier work, and route alternatives reduce dependence on precise platform timing. Keyboard menu navigation is supported. Comfort, readability and full mouse interaction still require player verification.

## Iteration and evidence

The user requested a stronger opening, less generic typography, a prettier sky, enjoyable sounds and a comfortable 3D view. Technical checks also found tutorial bypasses, invalid-height interactions, ramp lips, misleading side-route navigation, default-grid terrain and an unbuilt-lighting warning. Implementations and observed regressions are recorded in ITERATIONS.md and QA-Evidence.json. The user reported practice confusion and faint route markings on November 5. Those partial-session observations drove v0.5.3. A complete first-time run and measured 5–10 minute pacing remain required.


## v0.5 moment-to-moment clarity

In practice, cross after the cart has passed into its depot. Q is an optional tool for making the gap last longer. A safe crossing is accepted whether Q was held, released early, or never pressed. The lesson does not reset the courier for choosing natural timing. An actual cart hit knocks the courier back with a short stagger and an explicit message (v0.8; earlier versions returned to a checkpoint). Crossing saves forward progress.

One continuous orange line with broad arrows marks the single recommended route of the current task; side paths keep their signs but are not painted, so the line and the marker never disagree. Where the route continues on a lift or the shuttle, orange chevrons are painted on the platform and ride with it. A stationary orange ring and downward pointer mark the next waypoint; it follows a side path only once the courier is clearly on it. Future delivery markings are hidden. These guides have no collision. C gently recenters the view.

At a lift, wait for it to come down without spending Q, then hold Q and walk aboard (Space also works) while it rests at your level. Once aboard, release Q so it carries you normally. At the far landing, walk forward to step off. Contextual prompts follow these stages and remain visible while Q is active. A nearby delivery desk shows ARRIVED and E Deliver package. Reading the next brief has no timer; only Accept delivery starts it.

Coffee delivery places hot cups on the counter and starts the strings. The second delivery brings the audience into the terrace and starts the bells. The last delivery reveals the moonlit screen, three-stamp ticket and wider concluding view. These are implemented feedback; their emotional impact requires player observation.


## Player, perspective and competition

The expected player uses a keyboard and mouse and may be unfamiliar with timing challenges or 3D camera control. The player controls one courier from a following third-person camera, with an avatar-based interaction model. The intended skills are observation, coordinated movement, landing judgment and deciding when to spend a short time-control window. There is one local single-player campaign. There are no opponents, multiplayer rules, leaderboards, difficulty levels or separate time-trial mode. Improving one's own delivery time is an optional personal goal, not another implemented mode.

## Complete mode and transition reference

| Mode or shell screen | Available decisions and actions | Clock and devices | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening shell | Three story/control cards; continue/back; language; quit | No delivery timer; devices inactive | Continue after card three enters practice |
| Practice gameplay | Move, look, sprint, jump, slide, hold/release Q (unlimited), C, pause/help | No delivery deadline; all carts and lifts run; a striped yard gate stays closed | Actual jump and safe cart-lane crossing with or without Q; reaching the parcel desk opens the first brief |
| Await-pickup gameplay | Walk back to the desk after closing its brief; pause/help | No delivery deadline; carts and lifts keep running; only the marker to the desk is shown | Stepping up to the desk or pressing E there reopens the brief |
| Briefing shell | Read parcel, destination, route and budget; E or Enter accepts; Esc/Back closes | No budget consumed | Accept enters delivery; cancel returns to pickup |
| Delivery gameplay (primary) | Move/look/sprint/jump/climb/slide/Q; E delivers; C; help/pause; R asks to restart the current order | Current budget and marked devices advance in real time, with selective Q slowdown | Valid E delivery, timeout, or pause |
| Pause/help shell | Resume; story/route help; sound, camera and language settings; retry when available; quit | Clock, devices, charge and recovery are frozen | Resume returns to the exact preceding gameplay state |
| Delivered shell | View outcome/stamp, 1-3 stars, the customer's measure (warmth, lens condition or time left), hourglasses and sparks collected; read next brief | Completed delivery fixed; next timer has not started | Read next opens next brief at the same counter |
| Failed shell (time ran out, or the lens case cracked) | Retry unfinished order; view route help | No ongoing delivery or device movement; the screen names the cause | Retry reopens that order's brief at its pickup with a full clock, full Q uses, a fresh lens case and its cells back |
| Completion shell | Read ticket, stars per stamp, total out of 9 and rank, delivery time and retries; Play Again | Three deliveries complete; ending animation plays | Play Again returns to opening and resets progress |

Practice and delivery share the same avatar, camera and action vocabulary. Shell screens restrict world actions so reading and settings do not accidentally move the courier. Menu Tab selects controls; Space activates a focused toggle; Enter continues or accepts; sliders use Enter and arrows. A language change does not restart the order; the L shortcut works only on menu screens so it cannot fire mid-delivery. Progress is session-only: quitting does not save a campaign. Play Again preserves selected presentation preferences in the current process; a fresh launch defaults to English.

## Operational rules and edge cases

- Move relative to camera direction. Space jumps from the ground; holding it does not provide flight. Orange markers identify routes/interaction areas; blue clock tags identify devices affected by Q. A physical alternative route does not require a separate unlock.
- Q affects blue carts and platforms, not the courier, scenery or order deadline. Each delivery starts with a fixed number of uses (3, 4, 5), and each gold hourglass, and every 25 sparks, picked up on the way adds one; starting a slowdown spends one. A use lasts at most three seconds; releasing early ends it and starts a 1.5-second recharge. Pressing Q while recharging or with no uses left does nothing to the world but flashes the meter red, and an empty count also shows a message pointing to gaps and side paths. Retrying an order restores its uses. Holding through exhaustion does not automatically start another use: release and press again.
- On a moving platform, wait for it to reach the near landing, slow it if needed while boarding, release Q to travel at normal speed, and leave at the far landing. Holding Q throughout the ride makes travel slower. The longer side route trades travel time for easier geometry.
- E only reads or delivers within the marked area at a compatible height; standing underneath or far above a desk is invalid. An invalid E does not lose a parcel or advance an order. A reachable destination says ARRIVED and shows the delivery action.
- Coffee, lens and reel must be completed in that order. Accepting gives 40, 120 and 130 seconds respectively (v0.9; sprinting let the expert playthrough finish the coffee in 7.8 s of the previous 60, and the earlier 140/170/200 had left over two thirds of every clock unused). Reading a brief never starts its clock. Delivery succeeds when E is used at the destination before the order reaches zero. An expired order must be retried.
- A fall into water, off a raised walkway or out of the delivery's area returns the courier to a safe grounded checkpoint with a message naming the cause. Cart contact is a knock-back with a 0.7 s stagger and brief immunity, not a teleport. No stamp or parcel is removed.
- Carts, lifts and the shuttle run whenever the courier can move, including practice and waiting for a pickup; collisions count the same everywhere.
- Until the first order is accepted, a striped yard gate after the parcel desk is closed. Walking around it returns the courier to the desk with the message that the gate opens on acceptance.
- E never fails silently: away from a desk or destination it says where to go instead.
- People are solid, and large props stop the camera instead of letting it slide inside them; thin posts and floor markings do not.
- Carts are traffic: each lane has a depot shed at both ends and carts drive out of one, across the lane and into the other, one way. Q slows the cart's clock, so it waits in its depot longer.
- Gold hourglasses: 4, 5 and 7 on the three routes (one more in practice, which only teaches). They drift toward a courier who comes within about 3 m; touching one gives Q one more use in the running order, pops "+1 borrowed time" and flashes the Q key. Most sit on the harder line. Retrying an order restores them.
- Gold sparks are laid every 2.5 m along each recommended line, along the market roof and along the springboard's flight. They drift in from about 2.4 m; each run of pickups raises the chime's pitch. Every 25 sparks give one more Q use (a small gold bar under the Q pips shows progress). A close call adds sparks: sliding through a shutter while it is below 165 cm (+3), clearing a turnstile arm (+2), crossing a lane within 3 m of a moving cart without touching it (+2). Retrying an order restores its sparks and empties the bar.
- Parkour: Shift raises run speed from 5.2 to 8.2 m/s. Ctrl starts a 0.75 s slide (from 10.5 m/s easing to 3.8 m/s) that halves the courier's height to 88 cm; the courier stands again as soon as there is headroom. A jump that meets a ledge 35-230 cm above the feet on a walkable surface (crates, decks, planters; never hedges, sheds or roofs that leave the level) climbs it in about a third of a second. A jump pressed up to 0.14 s after running off an edge still works, and one pressed up to 0.16 s before landing fires on touchdown.
- Rolling shutters (blue, clock-tagged, Q slows them): open 40% of each cycle, roll down over 22%, stay shut 18%, roll up 20%. A shut door is solid and harmless; a door coming down on a standing courier knocks them back (and knocks the lens case). A slide fits while the door is above about 95 cm.
- Turnstiles (blue, Q slows them): two arms on one hub, a low arm (35-75 cm) to jump and a high arm (110-150 cm) to slide under. Being swept is a knock-back like a cart. Springboards (orange, not affected by Q) launch a courier who runs across them about 5.6 m up, carrying their run speed.
- Stars, judged by each customer's concern: coffee by warmth (time left / limit: at least 45% earns 3, at least 20% earns 2, otherwise 1); the lens by care (no knocks 3, one knock 2, two knocks 1; a third knock cracks it); the reel by time left before the screening (same thresholds as coffee). The ending totals them out of 9: 9 Legend of the Last Minute, 7-8 Star Courier, 4-6 Reliable Courier, otherwise Courier in Training.
- Feel: holding Q tints the screen cool blue with a vignette and lowers the music's pitch; a cart hit or fall flashes red with a thud; the last 10 seconds tick; deliveries burst into paper confetti.
- R (after confirmation) and the Retry control reset the current unfinished order to its pickup, including its deadline, device positions and Q allowance. Earlier completed orders, stamps, cups, audience and music progression remain. Retry is unavailable during untimed practice and does not undo delivered or completed states.
- The ending's delivery time is the sum of the three successful-order clocks. It excludes story reading, practice, pause, brief recovery and discarded attempts. The Retry counter counts explicit order restarts, not individual falls/cart recoveries. Neither statistic is a complete first-time wall-clock duration.
- There is no life limit, combat, inventory puzzle, hidden score threshold, random failure roll, currency, paid unlock or permanently lost progress within a running session. No special ending requires a fast time.

## Ability teaching and later use

| Ability | Initial demonstrated use | Later available transfer | Validation still needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move/look | Reach the practice marker with camera-relative WASD; recenter and mouse controls explained | Choose long arcades or short device routes, turn between landings and align with the final cinema aisle | First-time route comprehension without coaching |
| Sprint | Hold Shift to reach the first marker | Outrun a closing shutter, carry speed into a slide, and carry it over the springboard to the terrace | Comfort at sprint speed |
| Jump / climb | Actually jump over the low training step; simply walking around does not pass. Climbing is introduced on the first crate wall with a contextual hint | Jump across the stepping stones; jump the turnstile's low arm while it sweeps; climb crates as a staircase onto the market roof; catch the terrace edge after the springboard | A first-time player must demonstrate a later application; safe routes can bypass these challenges |
| Slide | Slide under the striped practice beam, which spans hedge to hedge and is too tall to hop, so the lesson cannot be skipped | Slide under a rolling shutter while it is closing (timing, not just height); slide under a turnstile's high arm while it moves | Whether first-time players find Ctrl comfortable |
| Selective time control | Observe the cart gap; optionally try Q to prolong it. Safe natural timing is accepted | Slow a lift/shuttle for boarding, release it to travel, then judge disembarkation; slow a closing shutter so a slide fits under it; slow a turnstile to read its two arms; earn extra uses with sparks and hourglasses and decide where to spend them on the last route | Platform physics regression passes; ordinary simultaneous Q + movement input and independent human learning transfer still require testing |

The level provides later applications of every taught ability, and the painted recommended line uses them: the crate walls, the shutters, the turnstiles, the lifts, the stepping stones, the cart lanes and the shuttle. Side paths remain for cautious players; Borrower cells on the harder line and the star rating give a concrete reason to take the applications instead.

## Feedback-driven revisions

Ordinary keyboard checks in v0.5.1 led to a shorter coffee awning, open curb/rail junctions, safe side-ramp checkpoints and recovery guidance. v0.5.2 added staged platform instructions but also forced Q use at the training crossing. On November 5 the user reported that waiting for a natural gap was intuitive, yet crossing that way caused respawns; they also found the route unclear. v0.5.3 removes the forced-Q reset, distinguishes real collision recovery, saves crossing progress and adds continuous high-contrast route arrows plus a visible next-waypoint marker. On November 7 a real-speed autoplay that uses only player controls timed out twice at the final shuttle: a side-path guardrail fenced its boarding edge. v0.5.4 keeps rails off every platform footprint and the stepping-stone takeoff, adds a landing lip at the raised final lift, tightens the order budgets to 60/150/160 s after the expert run left most of each clock unused, and aligns two waypoints with painted corners. On November 8 the project owner's first uncoached playthrough of the packaged game found that the desk did not stop them, carts stood still before an order, E gave no response at the counter, and the camera could enter an NPC; they also asked for a visible Q count. v0.6 opens briefs on arrival, adds a yard gate until the first order, runs all devices continuously, explains every E press, gives people and props camera collision, and limits Q to a counted 3/4/5 uses per delivery. The owner then judged it still less fun than commercial games: v0.7 adds golden clocks on the risky lines, a 1-3 star rating per delivery with a total and rank, and audiovisual feel for Q, hits, the final seconds and deliveries. The owner then found it monotonous and often against common sense, and saw the orange line lead to a ramp while the marker led to a lift. v0.8 turns carts into one-way depot traffic, replaces teleporting hits with a knock-back, gives each parcel its own rule and stars (cooling coffee, a lens that cracks after three knocks, a reel racing the screening), turns golden clocks into Borrower cells (+1 Q use), adds hedges and named reset causes, explains each hand-over in the briefs, and paints only the recommended route with chevrons riding on the lifts. The owner then reported that people avoided the Borrower cell because it was the same blue as the hazards, that the game was still boring, and that it was too easy to finish, and suggested borrowing from parkour games. v0.9 makes the reward a gold hourglass that drifts toward the courier, adds lines of gold sparks and close-call bonuses, adds sprint, slide, ledge climbing and forgiving jumps, adds rolling shutters and turnstiles that combine Q with those moves plus a springboard, crate walls and a market roof, teaches the slide with an unskippable practice beam, and tightens the clocks to 40/120/130 s. A full consistency pass in the same version replaced stale "cart moves aside" hints, fixed key hints overlapping button captions and removed 42 unused translations.

Four engine regression suites pass (v0.9 adds Courier.Regression.Parkour), including safe crossings with no Q, early Q release, valid slowdown, real cart contact and route visibility/collision checks. An ordinary-keyboard technical check crossed the practice lane without Q and observed the next lesson with a full charge. These are partial technical checks and actual user-reported practice feedback. No complete human duration, enjoyment, comfort or later learning-transfer result has been recorded. Full observations and commit-linked changes are in ITERATIONS.md, PlaytestFeedback-Nov05.zh.md and QA-Evidence.json.
